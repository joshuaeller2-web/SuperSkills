# research-brief

Define the question, intended decision and relevant time window. Resolve the entity and inspect primary sources first. Use community discussion when sentiment or experience matters; popularity alone is not evidence quality. Select relevant research channels rather than forcing a fixed seven-channel sweep.

Record source URL, publication date and event date where different. Deduplicate repeated reporting without pretending syndicated copies are independent corroboration. Distinguish supported findings, credible disagreement and unverified claims. Never manufacture a quote, citation, handle or engagement count.

Deliver the answer with linked evidence, coverage limitations and unresolved questions. Name the specific missing record that would resolve each material gap, such as a contemporaneous announcement, shipment record, filing, archived page, or dated release artifact. For chronology, distinguish event time from report time and explain uncertain ordering. Use the installed super-video-research skill when video evidence matters and archify when an actual diagram helps. Research and drafting do not authorize sending, publishing or account changes.
